# Series of Fano quiver moduli from extended Dynkin quivers 

Pieter Belmans Markus Reineke October 16, 2026 

Abstract 

Associated to small extended Dynkin quivers, we construct series of Fano varieties of fixed Picard rank at most five and arbitrary even dimension, which admit a very explicit description of their geometry. For example, they all admit a flat morphism to a projective space, whose general fiber is a product of projective lines. We also give a closed formula for all Betti numbers, and interpret this formula in terms of an action of a cohomological Hall algebra on the cohomology of all the varieties in a series.

# 1 Introduction 

We consider extended Dynkin quivers together with multiples of their unique imaginary Schur root, and study the framed quiver moduli one can attach to this setting. Our first result is that, under a natural symmetry condition on the Euler form, the morphism induced by forgetting the framing defines a flat map to a lower-dimensional projective space, making their geometry amenable to an explicit description. 1arXiv:2610.22203v1 [math.AG] 15 Oct 2026 We also describe when the natural stability parameter for a framed quiver moduli space is in the same chamber as the canonical stability parameter, making the framed quiver moduli space a Fano variety. This setting leads to an interesting and tractable classification problem, which results in five infinite series (and some sporadic cases, which we ignore). By considering all the varieties in an infinite series at once, we can exhibit closed expressions for their Betti numbers, and we uncover an action of a cohomological Hall algebra on the cohomology of the varieties to give a structural interpretation to this pattern for the Betti numbers. The intrinsic reason for this favourable behaviour is that, since our starting point is an extended Dynkin quiver, all relevant semistable quiver Donaldson-Thomas invariants can be computed and the corresponding cohomological Hall algebra can be described by explicit generators. The starting point for this classification and these results was the realization that one of the Fano fourfolds studied in [5], a priori defined using a subspace quiver, also admits an interpretation in terms of a framing of the extended D 4-quiver. It turns out that this alternative perspective using Dynkin quivers is the one that explains the interesting fibration structure described in §5 of op. cit. In this paper we greatly extend this geometric description in dimension 4 for a single quiver to a general framework in all even dimensions for framings of extended Dynkin quivers. This gives us an interesting class of quiver moduli with a particularly elegant and explicit geometry.

The Picard rank is then given in [11, Proposition 3.1]. The rationality of coprime quiver moduli is established in [22, Theorem 6.4]. The pure Hodge–Tate type is [14, Theorem 3]. An important caveat to make is that the standing assumption in [1, 2] is that of strong ample stability (or as explained in [1, Remark 1.3], it suffices that a certain inequality holds for every non-trivial Harder–Narasimhan type). This condition is replaced by the weaker condition of ample stability in [4].

3.1 Extended Dynkin quivers 

We recall and prove some basic results about extended Dynkin quivers which are necessary for the construction in Section 3.2. Let 𝑄 be an acyclic quiver of extended Dynkin (or Euclidean) type, so the underlying unoriented graph is an extended Dynkin diagram of type • eA𝑛 for 𝑛 ≥ 1, • eD𝑛 for 𝑛 ≥ 4, or • eE𝑛 for 𝑛 = 6, 7, 8. Because 𝑄 is of extended Dynkin type, the quadratic form attached to the symmetrization (− , −) of the Euler form is semi-positive definite, with radical generated by an indivisible vector 𝛿 ∈ N𝑄 0. We thus have (𝛿, −) = 0, and ⟨𝛿, 𝛿 ⟩ = 0. Following Dlab and Ringel [7] (see also [21, §3.5]), we define the defect function as 𝜕 = ⟨𝛿, −⟩ , thus we have {𝛿, −} = 2𝜕 . Lemma 6. Let 𝑄 be an extended Dynkin quiver. If a dimension vector 𝒆 ∈ Ker (𝜕 ) admits a 𝜕 -stable representation, then 𝒆 = 𝛿 or 𝒆 ∈ 𝑃 . Definition 23. Let 𝑆 be a smooth variety. An involution surface bundle is a flat projective mor-phism 𝑝 : 𝑋 → 𝑆 for which • the locus 𝑈 ⊆ 𝑆 where 𝑝 is a smooth morphism is Zariski-dense, • the fibre 𝑝 −1 (𝑠 ) for 𝑠 ∈ 𝑈 is an involution surface, i.e., a minimal del Pezzo surface of degree 8.
