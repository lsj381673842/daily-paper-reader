COHEN-MACAULAY TRUNCATED PATH AND NAKAYAMA ALGEBRAS 

TAKAHIRO HONMA AND SATOSHI USUI ∗

Abstract. This paper characterizes Cohen-Macaulay algebras in the sense of Aus-lander and Reiten [ Progr. Math. 95(1991)] within the following two classes of finite dimensional algebras: (i) truncated path algebras and (ii) Nakayama algebras. For the former, our characterization in terms of ordinary quivers shows that if a Cohen-Macaulay truncated path algebra Λ is connected, then Λ or D(Λ) gives a dualizing module, where D denotes the standard duality. For the latter, we show that every Cohen-Macaulay Nakayama algebra Λ is Iwanaga-Gorenstein. In particular, Λ al-ways gives a dualizing module. Consequently, there is no non-trivial Cohen-Macaulay algebra among truncated path and Nakayama algebras. 

1. Introduction 

Let R be a commutative Cohen-Macaulay local ring of finite Krull dimension with a dualizing module (also called a canonical module) ω. Then Sharp [20, Theorem 3.11] proved that there are quasi-inverse equivalences 

− ⊗ R ω : P<∞(R) → I <∞(R) and Hom R(ω, −) : I<∞(R) → P <∞(R),(1.0.1) where P<∞(R) (resp. I<∞(R)) denotes the category of finitely generated R-modules of finite projective (resp. injective) dimension. Later, as a non-commutative analogue of commutative Cohen-Macaulay rings, Auslander and Reiten [4] introduced Cohen-Macaulay algebras , namely, artin algebras Λ admitting a Λ-bimodule W , called a du-alizing module , which induces the same quasi-inverse equivalences as in (1.0.1). It is known that a dualizing module is unique up to isomorphism as a one-sided module but not as a bimodule; see Remark 2.5. Also, there are other non-commutative analogues of commutative Cohen-Macaulay rings; see [7, 6, 12]. As in the case of commutative rings, Iwanaga-Gorenstein algebras [10], which form a non-commutative analogue of commutative Gorenstein rings, are precisely the Cohen-Macaulay algebras Λ for which the Λ-bimodule Λ gives a dualizing module. Besides, we have the following examples of Cohen-Macaulay algebras, where the first is due to Auslander and Reiten [4], the second to Nishida [18], and the last two to Chan, Iyama and Marczinzik [8]: (1) artin algebras Λ such that the finitistic dimensions of Λ and Λ op are zero;

(2) the artin algebra Λ given in [18, Example 1]; (3) contracted preprojective algebras of Dynkin type; (4) tensor products of finite dimensional Cohen-Macaulay algebras over a field K.These seem to be all known examples so far. The algebras in (1) contain local algebras and are precisely the Cohen-Macaulay algebras Λ for which the Λ-bimodule D(Λ) gives a dualizing module, where D denotes the Matlis duality. Therefore, it is natural to try to find Cohen-Macaulay algebras Λ for which neither Λ nor D(Λ) gives a dualizing module, more essentially, those for which a dualizing module, regarded as a one-sided module, admits a direct summand that is neither projective nor injective. In fact, the algebra in (2) and some of the algebras in (3) are such non-trivial Cohen-Macaulay algebras. In this paper, we characterize the Cohen-Macaulayness of two classes of finite dimen-sional algebras. First, motivated by the example of Chan, Iyama and Marczinzik [8, Example 1.3(1)] showing that radical square zero truncated path algebras KQ/ ⟨all paths of length 2 ⟩ of quivers Q without sink or source are Cohen-Macaulay algebras in (1), we focus on the truncated path algebras Λ = KQ/ ⟨all paths of length L + 1 ⟩ of connected quivers Q,where L ≥ 1. After introducing the notion of balanced quivers (Definition 3.1), we show in Theorem 3.3 that Λ is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if Q is balanced. In this case, Λ or D(Λ) gives a dualizing module. Thus, there is no non-trivial Cohen-Macaulay truncated path algebra. This is our first main result. Note that the connectedness of 

Q is used only to simplify the statement; see Corollary 3.5 for the case where Q is not necessarily connected. Also, Chan, Iyama and Marczinzik [8] introduced a class of Cohen-Macaulay algebras, called minimal Auslander-Cohen-Macaulay algebras , as a generalization of minimal Auslander-Gorenstein algebras [14]. In Corollary 3.8, we show that among truncated path algebras, minimal Auslander-Cohen-Macaulay algebras are precisely the minimal Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras. Second, we consider Nakayama algebras. As our second main result, we show in Theorem 3.9 that a Nakayama algebra is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if it is Iwanaga-Gorenstein. Thus, there is no non-Iwanaga-Gorenstein Cohen-Macaulay Nakayama algebra; in other words, we can always choose Λ as a dualizing module over a Cohen-Macaulay Nakayama algebra Λ. Consequently, there is no non-trivial Cohen-Macaulay algebra among truncated path and Nakayama algebras. This paper is organized as follows. We first recall some basic notions and results related to Cohen-Macaulay algebras in Section 2. We then prove our main results in Section 3.

2.2. Cohen-Macaulay algebras. We begin with the definition of Cohen-Macaulay algebras. 

Definition 2.3 ([4, p.223]) . An algebra Λ is called Cohen-Macaulay if there exists a Λ-bimodule W which gives quasi-inverse equivalences 

− ⊗ Λ W : P<∞(Λ) → I <∞(Λ) and Hom Λ(W, −) : I<∞(Λ) → P <∞(Λ) .

We call the Λ-bimodule W a dualizing module .From the definition, a direct product Λ 1 × · · · × Λn of algebras Λ i is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if each Λ i is Cohen-Macaulay. Auslander and Reiten characterised dualizing modules in terms of cotilting modules. 

Proposition 2.4 ([4, Proposition 1.3]) . A Λ-bimodule W is a dualizing module if and only if W is an Ext-maximal cotilting module as a left and as a right Λ-module, and the natural algebra morphism Λ → End( WΛ), given by x 7 → [w 7 → xw ], is an isomorphism. 

Remark 2.5. By Propositions 2.1 and 2.4, a dualizing module is uniquely determined up to isomorphism as a left and as a right module. However, it is not as a bimodule; see [4, p.226]. Proposition 2.6 ([4, Proposition 1.6]) . Let Λ be a Cohen-Macaulay algebra with du-alizing module W . Then fin . dim Λ = inj . dim Λ W = inj . dim Λop W = fin . dim Λ op .

An algebra Λ is called Iwanaga-Gorenstein [10] if Λ has finite injective dimension as a left and as a right Λ-module. Iwanaga-Gorenstein algebras are precisely the Cohen-Macaulay algebras Λ whose dualizing modules are given by Λ. As the following propo-sition shows, the finiteness of the projective dimension of a dualizing module on one side is enough to characterize the Iwanaga-Gorensteinness of Cohen-Macaulay algebras. 

Proposition 2.7. The following conditions are equivalent for a Cohen-Macaulay alge-bra Λ with dualizing module W .

(1) Λ is an Iwanaga-Gorenstein algebra. 

(2) Λ has finite injective dimension as a left or as a right Λ-module. 

(3) W has finite projective dimension as a left or as a right Λ-module.

(1) follows from [3, Proposition 6.10] and Proposition 2.6. To prove (3) ⇒ (2), assume (3) holds. Then, since D(Λ) ∈ \add W from the definition of cotilting modules, we can conclude that D(Λ) has finite projective dimension on one side. □
