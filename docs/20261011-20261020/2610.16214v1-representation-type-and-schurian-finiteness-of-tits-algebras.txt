Representation Type and Schurian Finiteness of Tits Algebras     

> SIYUAN CHEN
> Abstract. We determine the representation type and Schurian finiteness of the Tits algebra of a finite central real hyperplane arrangement over an algebraically closed field. The algebra is representation-finite when the arrangement has at most two hyperplanes, tame when it has three, and wild when it has at least four. Moreover, it is Schurian-finite if and only if the arrangement has at most two hyperplanes. Both classifications hold over algebraically closed fields of arbitrary characteristic. The proof combines Saliola’s bound-quiver presentation with explicit module con-structions, convex subquivers, essentialization, and deletion. In the R2case, we describe all eleven indecomposable modules for two lines and construct a full exact embedding of modules over a free algebra for at least four lines.

1. Introduction 

The face semigroup of a real hyperplane arrangement encodes the geometry of its faces by a noncommutative multiplication, and its semigroup algebra is called the Tits algebra. One motivation for studying this algebra comes from random walks on chambers: Brown and Dia-conis [4] used the face action to determine stationary distributions and study the spectra and convergence of these walks. From a representation-theoretic perspective, Saliola [11] determined the quiver and relations of the face semigroup algebra, constructed its primitive idempotents, and proved that it is Koszul and depends only on the intersection lattice. These results turn questions about an algebra defined geometrically into explicit problems about a finite bound quiver. They also make it natural to ask how the combinatorics of an arrangement controls the complexity of its module category. Further motivation comes from the representation theory of Solomon descent algebras. For the braid arrangement, the symmetric-group invariant subalgebra of the Tits algebra is anti-isomorphic to the Solomon descent algebra [11]. Schocker [13] studied the full Solomon–Tits algebra of the symmetric group, describing its simple and projective indecomposable modules, Cartan invariants, and Ext-quiver. More recently, Erdmann and Lim [7] classified the rep-resentation type of the type-A descent algebras in positive characteristic. In a subsequent preprint [8], they determine the representation type of the Solomon descent algebras for the other irreducible finite Coxeter types, with type E8 remaining open. These developments mo-tivate the corresponding classification problem for the full Tits algebra of an arbitrary central arrangement and the comparison with Schurian finiteness. In particular, one can ask how both properties depend on the number and relative positions of the hyperplanes. In this paper, we study two such measures of complexity: representation type and Schurian finiteness. The finite–tame–wild classification concerns all finite-dimensional indecomposable modules, whereas Schurian finiteness concerns those nonzero modules whose endomorphism algebra consists only of scalars. We use Saliola’s presentation to classify the rank-two algebras, including an explicit list of eleven indecomposables for two lines and a full exact embedding

For three independent hyperplanes, the algebra is the incidence algebra of the Boolean lattice B3, whose tameness is recalled by Chapoton and Rognerud [5, Lemma 3.5]. Together with the four-hyperplane cases, essentialization and deletion yield the general classification. In particular, every central arrangement with at least four distinct hyperplanes has a wild Tits algebra, while Schurian finiteness holds precisely when there are at most two hyperplanes. The tame cases already have infinitely many Schurian modules, so the two classifications capture different features of the module category. The proof proceeds from small configurations to general arrangements. Section 2 develops the bound-quiver and quotient tools, and Section 3 treats the planar algebras by explicit module constructions. Sections 4 and 5 then use the geometry of three and four hyperplanes to identify suitable convex subquivers. Finally, Section 6 separates two complementary reductions: essen-tialization removes redundant ambient directions without changing the Tits algebra, whereas deletion produces quotient algebras attached to smaller arrangements. These reductions allow the wildness and Schurian infiniteness detected in the small configurations to pass to arbitrary central arrangements, explaining why a finite list of geometric cases suffices for the classification. We can now state the main results for essential arrangements of dimension at least two; the low-rank and nonessential cases are included in Section 6. 

Theorem 1.1. Let K be an algebraically closed field, and let A = {H1, . . . , H m} be a central essential arrangement of m distinct hyperplanes in Rn, where n ≥ 2. Write TA := K[F(A)] for its Tits algebra. 

(1) Suppose that n = 2 . Then m ≥ 2, and: 

(a) If m = 2 , then TA is representation-finite. 

(b) If m = 3 , then TA is of tame representation type. 

(c) If m ≥ 4, then TA is of wild representation type. 

(2) Suppose that n = 3 .

(a) If m = 3 , then TA is of tame representation type. 

(b) If m ≥ 4, then TA is of wild representation type. 

(3) Suppose that n ≥ 4. Then TA is of wild representation type. Furthermore, TA is Schurian-finite if and only if n = 2 and m = 2 .

Theorem 6.3. Let A be a central arrangement containing at least four distinct hyperplanes. Then TA is wild. Proof. By Lemma 6.1, we may assume that A is essential. Essentialization does not identify distinct hyperplanes, since each Hi is the inverse image of Hi/X under the quotient map. Let
