> Abstract. P-objects were introduced by Huybrechts and Thomas as a gener-alization of spherical objects. We classify the weight structures and t-structures on the triangulated category generated by a Pn[k]-object in the sense of Krug. For n= 1, this recovers the classification of weight structures and t-structures on the triangulated category generated by a spherical object due to Holm, Jør-gensen and Yang. We also compute the Koszul-dual model of the triangulated category generated by a Pn[k]-object.

1. Introduction 

Weight structures and t-structures are standard tools for working with trian-gulated categories, with many applications in representation theory, algebraic ge-ometry, and other areas. However, the classification of all weight structures and 

t-structures on a given triangulated category is usually not feasible. One interest-ing case where this has been achieved is the triangulated category generated by a spherical object. Spherical objects appear naturally in both algebraic geometry and representation theory, and the triangulated category they generate has been stud-ied in many different settings, see the introduction of [HJY13] for an overview. In [HJY13] Holm, Jørgensen and Yang classify the weight structures and t-structures on the triangulated category generated by a k-spherical object. More precisely, they show that for k > 0 there is no non-trivial weight structure and a unique (up to shift) non-trivial t-structure, while for k ≤ 0 there is no non-trivial t-structure and a unique (up to shift) non-trivial weight structure. A generalization of spherical objects are the P-objects introduced by Huybrechts and Thomas [HT06]. Following Krug [Kru18], we call an object P of a trian-gulated category C a Pn[k]-object if its total endomorphism algebra End ∗

k-spherical object. The main feature of P-objects and spherical objects is that they give rise to interesting autoequivalences known as P-twists [HT06], respectively spherical twists [ST01]. 

P-objects occur most naturally in algebraic geometry, see for instance [HT06, Ex. 1.3] and the survey in [Add16, §4.2]. In particular, by [Kru18, Observation 1.2] a compact Kähler manifold X of dimension 2 n is hyperkähler if and only if OX

Pn[k]-objects also appear in representation theory, but so far they have rarely been studied from this perspective. By [BC25, Thm. Furthermore, it is easy to see that the free dg module of rank 1 over the dg algebra 

A = k[x]/(xn+1 ) with |x| = k and trivial differential is a Pn[k]-object in perf (A). This is actually the “universal” Pn[k]-object: as a consequence of the formality result from [HK19], if P is a Pn[k] in an algebraic triangulated category C then Hom C (P, −) : thick C (P ) → perf (A) is an equivalence taking P to A. In partic-ular, the triangulated category generated by a Pn[k]-object is independent of the ambient triangulated category, see Section 2.2 for details. Our main result classifies the weight structures and t-structures on the triangu-lated category generated by a Pn[k]-object. 

Theorem A (Theorem 3.3) . Let T be the triangulated category generated by a 

Pn[k]-object. 1) For n = 0 , T admits a standard weight structure and a standard t-structure. Any non-trivial t-structure or weight structure on T is a shift of these. 2) For n > 0 and k > 0, T admits a standard t-structure, and any non-trivial 

t-structure on T is a shift of this. T does not admit a non-trivial weight structure. 3) For n > 0 and k ≤ 0, T admits a standard weight structure, and any non-trivial weight structure on T is a shift of this. T does not admit a non-trivial t-structure. 

For n = 0 the classification is well-known since in this case T is just Db(mod fd -k). For n = 1, Theorem A recovers [HJY13, Thm. A]. The proof of Theorem A occupies most of Section 3. Standard results from silting theory provide the standard weight structure (for k ≤ 0) respectively t-structure (for k > 0), see Section 3.2. The main idea for the classification is to show that if 

X ∈ T does not lie in the standard (co)heart up to shift, then Hom T (X, X [r]) ̸ = 0 for some r > 0 (if k ≤ 0) respectively r < 0 (if k > 0). From this it follows that any non-zero (co)heart must already contain a shift of P . The required morphisms are constructed separately for k < 0, k = 0, and k > 0, although the arguments for 

k < 0 and k = 0 are very similar. For k > 0 our argument is “model-free” and only uses the standard t-structure, while for k ≤ 0 we explicitly use that T is equivalent to perf (A) where A = k[x]/(xn+1 ) with |x| = k and trivial differential. Finally, t-structures and weight structures with zero (co)heart are easily seen to be trivial, see Proposition 3.4. The non-existence of t-structures respectively weight structures follows from this and a well-known obstruction in terms of the the Calabi–Yau dimension, see Section 3.5. The fundamental idea of the proof (i.e. constructing morphisms that obstruct objects other than P from lying in (co)hearts) is the same as in [HJY13]. However, these morphisms are obtained differently. The approach in [HJY13] is to explicitly describe the entire triangulated category T by writing down the Auslander–Reiten quiver of T . The non-zero Hom spaces are computed via long exact sequences, and described combinatorially in terms of the Auslander–Reiten quiver. The classifica-tion of weight structures and t-structures can then essentially be read off. Instead of the “canonical” model T = perf (k[x]/(x2)) with |x| = k, these arguments use the Koszul-dual model: T is equivalent to thick( k) ⊆ D(k[t]), where |t| = 1 − k.This allows to explicitly list all indecomposable objects, as k[t] is graded hereditary. This approach does not work in general, as perf (k[x]/(xn+1 ) is wild for n > 1. Definition 3.1. A t-structure on a triangulated category D is a pair t = ( Dt≤0, Dt≥0)of strict full subcategories such that

4.3. The Koszul duality equivalence. For a proper non-positive dg algebra A,contravariant dg Koszul duality provides an equivalence perf (A)op → Dfd (( A!,dg )op ). To obtain similar equivalences if A is proper positive, the following property intro-duced in [Fus25, Def. 3.23] is required. There are several other equivalent charac-terizations, see [Fus25, Thm. 3.14]. Recall that the perfect derived category of a locally finite positive dg algebra A

admits a standard t-structure with ♡ = extclos( A) [KN13, Thm. 7.1]. 

Definition 4.14. A locally finite positive dg algebra A is pvd-finite if the heart of the standard t-structure on perf (A) has an injective cogenerator. 

Lemma 4.15. Let n > 0 and k > 0. The dg algebra A = k[x]/(xn+1 ) with |x| = k

is pvd-finite unless n = 1 and k = 1 .REFERENCES 19 

Proof. This follows directly from (the proof of) Lemma 3.11: for k > 1, the heart of the standard t-structure is semisimple, and hence the unique simple object A ∈ ♡ 

is an injective cogenerator. For k = 1 and n > 1 we have ♡ = add( {A, X 2}) where 

X2 is a length 2 extension of A with itself, and X2 is an injective cogenerator. For 

k = 1 and n = 1 we have ♡ = add( {Xi | i ≥ 1}) where Xi is uniserial with i many simple subquotients A, and none of the Xi is injective. □

It follows that dg Koszul duality yields an alternative model of the triangulated category generated by a Pn[k]-object. For n = 1 this is stated implicitly in [KYZ09, §2], cf. also [HJY13, §1.1].
