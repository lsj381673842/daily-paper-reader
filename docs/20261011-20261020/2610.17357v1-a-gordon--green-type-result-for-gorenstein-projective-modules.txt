# A Gordon–Green-type result for Gorenstein-projective modules 

A. M. Bouhada and M. Laaraj 

Abstract 

Let Λ be a Z-graded Artin algebra which is Iwanaga–Gorenstein. We prove that Λ is CM-finite if and only if the category of gradable Gorenstein-projective modules has finite type, and if and only if the lengths of the indecomposable gradable Gorenstein-projective mod-ules are bounded. Moreover, if Λ is CM-finite, then every Gorenstein-projective Λ-module is gradable. These results provide Gorenstein-projective analogues of the corresponding theorems of Gordon–Green for graded Artin algebras of finite representation type. 

Keywords: Gorenstein-projective modules; graded Artin algebras; gradable modules; Cohen– Macaulay finite type; Iwanaga–Gorenstein algebras. 

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 16G50; Secondary 16G60, 16G70, 16E65. 

# 1 Introduction 

Let Λ be a graded Artin algebra. Gordon and Green [11] studied Λ through the full subcate-gory mod ∞(Λ) of mod Λ consisting of gradable modules. Among their main results, they proved that Λ has finite representation type if and only if mod ∞(Λ) has finite representation type [11, Theorem 4.5], and that finite representation type implies that every Λ-module is gradable [11, Theorem 4.3]. Finite Cohen–Macaulay type and Gorenstein-projective modules have been studied from several complementary points of view. Chen [7] obtained a Gorenstein-projective analogue of Auslander’s theorem on algebras of finite representation type. Beligiannis [4, 5] developed a systematic study of virtually Gorenstein algebras and algebras of finite Cohen–Macaulay type. Further results on CM-finite Gorenstein algebras and constructions of Gorenstein-projective modules were obtained by Li and Zhang [15, 16], while Kong and Zhang [14] investigated relative Auslander algebras associated with CM-finite algebras. In the graded setting, Minamoto and Yamaura [17] studied finitely graded Iwanaga–Gorenstein algebras and the stable categories of their graded Cohen–Macaulay modules. Explicit descriptions are available for important classes of algebras. In particular, Chen, Shen and Zhou [8] classified the indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein-projective modules over a monomial algebra in terms of perfect paths. More recently, Honma and Usui [13] described both the stable category of Gorenstein-projective modules and the stable category of graded Gorenstein-projective modules over a monomial algebra, together with the relation between them via the orbit category with respect to the degree-shift functor. These results illustrate the close interaction between grading and Gorenstein-projective representation theory. The question considered here is different. We study how CM-finiteness interacts with grad-ability and ask whether the finiteness phenomena established by Gordon and Green for ordinary modules continue to hold inside the Gorenstein-projective category. Recall that Λ is CM-finite 

if Gproj Λ has only finitely many indecomposable objects up to isomorphism. The following main theorem shows that the Gordon–Green finiteness phenomenon extends to the Gorenstein-projective setting. 

Theorem 1.1 (Main Theorem) . Let Λ be a graded Artin algebra which is Iwanaga–Gorenstein. The following conditions are equivalent: 

1arXiv:2610.17357v1 [math.RA] 12 Oct 2026 A Gordon–Green-type result A. M. Bouhada and M. Laaraj 

(1) Λ is CM-finite. 

(2) The category Gproj ∞(Λ) of gradable Gorenstein-projective modules has finite type. 

(3) The lengths of the indecomposable gradable Gorenstein-projective modules are uniformly bounded. 

(4) GG < ∞ and Gproj d(Λ) has finite type for every d ≥ 0.Moreover, if Λ is CM-finite, then every Gorenstein-projective Λ-module is gradable. Con-sequently, the ungraded stable category of Gorenstein-projective modules can be realized as the orbit category of the graded stable category of graded Gorenstein-projective modules with respect to the degree-shift functor ⟨1⟩. In particular, one has GprojΛ ≃ Gproj ZΛ/⟨1⟩ .

The notation appearing in (4) is introduced in Section 2. When Λ is self-injective, every finitely generated Λ-module is Gorenstein-projective, and the theorem recovers the correspond-ing finiteness phenomena of Gordon and Green. 

Strategy of the proof. We combine right almost split morphisms in mod Λ with Gorenstein-projective approximations. In the graded setting, these approximations are chosen so that gradability is preserved, yielding right almost split morphisms in Gproj Λ whose domains are gradable whenever their targets are. Iterating the indecomposable components of these mor-phisms produces chains in Gproj Λ, along which gradability propagates from the last term to all preceding terms. Under the bounded-length hypothesis in (3), an expansion argument, together with the Harada–Sai lemma, shows that every indecomposable Gorenstein-projective module is, up to isomorphism, the initial term of a chain of uniformly bounded length ending at an indecomposable projective module. This yields both CM-finiteness and the gradability of all Gorenstein-projective modules. Our approach is inspired by Auslander’s use of the Harada–Sai lemma to show that a component of the Auslander–Reiten quiver consisting of modules of bounded length contains all indecomposable modules [2, Chapter VI]; a relative version appears in [5, Remark 3.2(ii)]. Unlike the approach of Gordon and Green [11], our proof uses neither Auslander’s theorem on components nor the connectedness of Λ. The essential additional ingredient in our setting is the propagation of gradability along the chosen chains, which allows the length bound to be imposed only on gradable modules. The same argument also provides a short proof of the results of Gordon and Green [11, Theorems 4.3 and 4.5]. Indeed, working in mod Λ instead of Gproj Λ, neither Gorenstein-projective approximations nor the Gorenstein hypothesis are needed. Moreover, the chains may be taken to end at simple modules rather than projective ones, since every nonzero module admits a nonzero morphism onto a simple module, and there are only finitely many simple modules, all of which are gradable.

Corollary 2.20. In Setting 2.18, if Λ is CM-finite, then every Gorenstein-projective Λ-module is gradable. Proof. Let G be an indecomposable Gorenstein-projective Λ-module. By Lemma 2.19, sG is indecomposable and Gorenstein-projective for every s ∈ k∗. Since Λ is CM-finite, the family 

{sG : s ∈ k∗} contains only finitely many isomorphism classes. Since k is algebraically closed, k∗ is infinite. Hence there is some α ∈ k∗ such that αG ∼= β G

for infinitely many β ∈ k∗. By [1, Theorem 4.2], this is impossible unless G is gradable. Thus every indecomposable Gorenstein-projective module is gradable. Since mod Λ is Krull–Schmidt and finite direct sums of gradable modules are gradable, every Gorenstein-projective Λ-module is gradable.
