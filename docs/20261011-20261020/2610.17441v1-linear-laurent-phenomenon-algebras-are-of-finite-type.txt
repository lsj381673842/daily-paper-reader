# Linear Laurent phenomenon algebras are of finite type 

Oliver Daisey 

oliverjdaisey@gmail.com 

Abstract 

Laurent phenomenon algebras generalise cluster algebras by allowing irreducible exchange polynomials in place of binomials. Lam and Pylyavskyy proved finite type for those arising from graphs and conjectured that the same holds for every Laurent phenomenon algebra admitting a linear seed. We prove this conjecture over an arbitrary unique factorisation domain. Our proof constructs a nonzero element of the upper algebra divisible by every cluster variable. Comparing noninitial cluster variables with the prime factors of this element in the initial Laurent ring bounds the number of cluster variable classes and yields a finite normalised mutation pattern.

# 1 Introduction 

Laurent phenomenon algebras (LP algebras) were introduced by Lam and Pylyavskyy in 2012 [LP16a] to generalise Fomin and Zelevinsky’s cluster algebras. Introduced in [FZ02], cluster algebras provide a combinatorial framework for studying total positivity and canonical bases. Their generators are grouped into clusters, and mutation exchanges one variable at a time using an exchange binomial. Among the central results of the theory are the Laurent phenomenon and the finite-type classification by Dynkin diagrams [FZ03]. LP algebras allow arbitrary irreducible exchange polynomials in place of binomials. This greater freedom retains the Laurent phenomenon, but makes the mutation rule and the 1arXiv:2610.17441v1 [math.CO] 12 Oct 2026 question of finite type more delicate. In rank two, Lam and Pylyavskyy give a complete finite-type classification [LP16a, Theorem 6.4]. In higher rank, they prove finite type for the graph LP algebras, whose initial exchange polynomials have independent constant terms and variable coefficients in {0, 1} [LP16b]. They conjecture more generally that every LP algebra admitting a linear seed is of finite type [LP16b, Section 1.7 and Conjecture 7.5]. In this paper, we prove this conjecture. ## 2.3 LP algebras, equivalence, and finite type 

Definition 2.6 (Seed equivalence) . Two seeds are equivalent if, after appropriately matching cluster variables to exchange polynomials in each seed, corresponding variables and exchange polynomials are associated over A as elements of F. We write [ S] for the equivalence class of 

S.When coordinates have been rescaled, their polynomial expressions are interpreted in the common ambient field. Mutation respects this equivalence [LP16a, Lemma 3.1]. 

Definition 2.7 (Laurent phenomenon algebra) . A Laurent phenomenon algebra A = ( A, S)consists of a connected mutation pattern of seeds S in F, closed under mutation with specified unit choices, together with the A-subalgebra A ⊆ F generated by its cluster variables. The notation A(S) indicates a chosen initial seed S in this pattern. Equivalently one may first work with the mutation class up to seed equivalence. Multiplying generators by coefficient units does not change the generated A-algebra. Connectedness excludes a disjoint union of unrelated mutation classes. 

Proposition 2.8 (Laurent phenomenon) . For any seed T of an LP algebra A, every cluster variable is an element of L(T ). In particular, A ⊆ L (T ).Proof. This is [LP16a, Theorem 5.1]. 

Definition 2.9 (Normalisation and finite type) . A pattern is normalised if it contains exactly one representative of each of its seed-equivalence classes. A normalisation of a pattern is a mutation-compatible choice of equivalent representative seeds. An LP algebra is of finite type 

if it admits a normalisation with finitely many seeds. 5Note that choosing arbitrary representatives does not by itself check closure under the exact mutation rule f ii = f i. We shall make a compatible choice in Proposition 5.6. In rank two, if the exchange degrees are b, c ≥ 0, finite type is equivalent to bc < 4 [LP16a, Theorem 6.4].
