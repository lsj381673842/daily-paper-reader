Abstract 

We relate multiplication formulas for regular modules in coefficient-free acyclic affine cluster algebras with skew-symmetric exchange matrices to the imaginary exchange relations of Reading –Stella[15]. Introducing a parameter z into the construction of Zhou –Zhu[17], we define a weighted cluster character on cluster tubes that satisfies generalized exchange relations of type Cn−1 and yields a bijection between indecom-posable rigid objects and cluster variables. Using the multiplication formulas and difference property of Ding –Xiao –Xu[5], we show that non-homogeneous tube em-beddings induce injective homomorphisms into ordinary affine cluster algebras under 

z 7 → Xδ. Comparison with Reading –Stella ’s relations identifies Xδ with the corre-sponding theta function, giving a representation-theoretic interpretation of the gen-eralized exchange parameter. 

Keywords: generalized cluster algebra; cluster tube; cluster category

# 1 Introduction 

The starting point of this paper is the observation that existing representation-theoretic formulas for affine cluster algebras are directly related to the imaginary exchange relations recently obtained by Reading–Stella. The multiplication formulas for regular modules and the difference property of Ding–Xiao–Xu yield three-term relations with Xδ as the middle coeffi-cient. Using a weighted cluster character on a cluster tube and the tube embedding, we realize this connection in the skew-symmetric coefficient-free setting as an injective homomorphism from a generalized cluster algebra to an ordinary affine cluster algebra. Cluster algebras were introduced by Fomin–Zelevinsky in their study of total positivity and canonical bases [6]. They generate cluster variables recursively by seed mutation. One 1arXiv:2610.21822v1 [math.RT] 15 Oct 2026 1 INTRODUCTION 2of the central problems in the theory is to realize these variables and their exchange relations geometrically or through representation theory. In the acyclic setting, cluster variables can be described by categorical objects, while mutation corresponds to the replacement of objects and to exchange triangles. The construction of cluster categories by Buan et al., together with Keller’s work on trian-gulated structures on orbit categories, laid the foundations for this approach [1, 9]. Caldero– Keller established correspondences between rigid objects and cluster variables, and between cluster-tilting objects and clusters [3]; Palu subsequently developed a more general cluster character [14]. Such formulas combine the index of an object with the Euler characteristics of submodule Grassmannians to turn categorical information into Laurent polynomials, with one-dimensional extension spaces giving rise to ordinary binomial exchange relations. Cluster tubes exhibit a different situation in the absence of cluster-tilting objects. A cluster tube of rank n > 1, denoted by Cn, is a 2-Calabi–Yau triangulated category that has maximal rigid objects but no cluster-tilting objects. Buan–Marsh–Vatne revealed the connection between its maximal rigid objects and cluster combinatorics of finite type [2]. Zhou–Zhu and Fu– Geng–Liu further constructed a map analogous to the Caldero–Chapoton formula, realizing a correspondence between indecomposable rigid objects and cluster variables of type Cn−1 [7, 17]. On the other hand, Chekhov–Shapiro introduced generalized cluster algebras in their study of surfaces with orbifold points, allowing exchange polynomials of higher degree to generalize ordinary binomial relations [4]. In a cluster tube, some exchange pairs have two-dimensional extension spaces, and the middle terms of their two exchange triangles are U ⊕2 and V ⊕2.These multiplicities naturally correspond to the square terms in a quadratic exchange relation; to obtain the exchange polynomial 1 + zu + u2, however, we are also expected to explain the origin of the middle term and the parameter z.A representation-theoretic approach to these generalized cluster algebras was developed by Labardini –Fragoso and Mou [11], who associate finite-dimensional gentle Jacobian algebras to triangulations of unpunctured surfaces with orbifold points of order three. Using stability scattering diagrams, they prove that the Caldero –Chapoton map gives a bijection between reachable τ -rigid pairs and cluster monomials of the associated generalized cluster algebra, with support τ -tilting mutation corresponding to seed mutation. The quadratic exchange polynomial arising at an orbifold point in their setting is 1 + u + u2, which is the specialization at z = 1 of the polynomial 1 + zu + u2 considered here. Affine representation theory provides a natural setting for this problem. Quasi-simple modules in homogeneous tubes whose dimension vector is the minimal positive imaginary root 

δ have a common cluster character, denoted by Xδ. In their study of integral bases, Ding–Xiao– Xu established multiplication formulas for regular modules and proved the difference property [5]. The latter relates objects in a non-homogeneous tube whose quasi-length equals its rank to Xδ. We observe that combining these earlier formulas and eliminating suitable non-rigid regular objects yields three-term relations with Xδ as the middle coefficient. Meanwhile, Gross–Hacking–Keel–Kontsevich developed a new framework for canonical bases by constructing theta functions via scattering diagrams [8]. Reading–Stella recently stud-ied theta functions on the imaginary wall in acyclic affine type, obtained imaginary exchange relations, and proved that tube subalgebras in the coefficient-free setting carry generalized clus-1 INTRODUCTION 3ter algebra structures of type C [15]. Our concern here is the precise correspondence between this new result and the earlier formulas for regular modules: once the notation for objects and indices is aligned, the two approaches give three-term relations of the same form, and their coefficients can be identified with one another. More precisely, let Q be a connected acyclic quiver of affine type other than the Kronecker quiver.
