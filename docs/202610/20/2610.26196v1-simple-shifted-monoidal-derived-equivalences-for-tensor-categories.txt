SIMPLE-SHIFTED MONOIDAL DERIVED EQUIVALENCES FOR TENSOR
CATEGORIES
JING YU
Abstract. In this paper, we investigate monoidal derived equivalences between tensor cat-
egories with infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects. We introduce the notion
of a simple-shifted monoidal derived equivalence and prove that a simple-shifted monoidal
derived equivalence gives rise to a tensor equivalence. In addition, we investigate which ten-
sor categories have the property that every monoidal derived equivalence between them is
necessarily simple-shifted, and we establish some sufficient conditions for this property. As
a corollary, we show that a monoidal derived equivalence between pointed tensor categories
implies a tensor equivalence between them.
1. Introduction
Monoidal triangulated categories arise naturally in stable homotopy theory, algebraic geom-
etry, and representation theory. In the setting of symmetric monoidal triangulated categories,
Balmer developed tensor triangular geometry, providing a systematic framework for study-
ing the prime tensor ideals and supports [4]. Nakano, Vashaw, and Yakimov extended this
framework to monoidal triangulated categories without assuming a braiding, establishing a
noncommutative theory of spectra and support with applications to finite-dimensional Hopf
algebras and small quantum Borel algebras [14]. Subsequent developments address the ten-
sor product property for cohomological support maps [15] and the relationship between the
cohomological support theory and the universal support theory based on the noncommuta-
tive Balmer spectra of the stable (triangulated) categories for finite tensor categories [16].
These developments illustrate how the interaction between tensor products and triangulated
structures reveals information that is not captured by the triangulated structure alone.
It is natural to ask to what extent an abelian category is determined by its derived cat-
egory. In general, derived equivalence does not imply equivalence of the underlying abelian
categories.
Rickard’s derived Morita theory [19] provides a systematic framework for un-
derstanding such equivalences through tilting complexes. For monoidal categories, however,
a derived equivalence preserving the monoidal structure imposes extra constraints and can
make reconstruction possible. In algebraic geometry, the term reconstruction theorem refers
to results that characterize this phenomenon; see [3, 8]. For algebras, Aihara and Mizuno [1]
proved that, among preprojective algebras of Dynkin type, derived equivalence forces Morita
equivalence. Zhang and Zhou [26] showed that an analogous phenomenon holds for finite-
dimensional hereditary weak bialgebras. Furthermore, Xu and Zheng [22] showed that, for
tensor categories with finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects, tensor equivalence
is equivalent to monoidal triangulated equivalence of their derived categories. In our previous
work [23], we studied reconstruction theorems for suitable abelian subcategories of tensor cat-
egories with infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects, using triangulated module
equivalences between triangulated module categories. More recently, Tan [20] established re-
construction for semisimple tensor categories with possibly infinitely many isomorphism classes

This paper is devoted to monoidal derived equivalences between tensor categories with
infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects. Note that in the case of finitely many
isomorphism classes of simple objects, Xu and Zheng showed that the presence of a monoidal
structure ensures that the heart of the standard t-structure is carried to the heart of the
standard t-structure. The central idea of their proof is that a derived equivalence sends the
standard t-structure to a new t-structure, and that any two bounded t-structures on the
derived category of an abelian category of finite length with finitely many isomorphism classes
of simple objects are related [11, Example 4.5]. The monoidal structure then exploits this
relation to force the new t-structure to coincide with the standard one. Our previous work
[23] was guided by the same idea.
A natural idea then arises: we still consider the t-exact functors in the sense of [10, Sub-
section 2.2], but instead of requiring that the heart of the standard t-structure be mapped
to the heart of the standard t-structure, we allow it to be mapped to the heart of another
t-structure. The question is then whether a monoidal derived equivalence still guarantees a
monoidal equivalence. In fact, Tan’s study of monoidal derived equivalences for semisimple
tensor categories ([20]) is precisely of this type. We also expect the rigidity assumption on
the tensor categories to play a genuine role in monoidal derived equivalences, so that such an
equivalence forces a tensor equivalence.
In this paper, we first introduce the notion of a simple-shifted functor (see Definition 3.1).
It is specified by a triple (F, ψ, r), where F : Db(A ) →Db(B) is a triangulated functor be-
tween bounded derived categories of abelian categories, ψ : SA →SB is a map of sets, and
r : SA →Z is a map of sets, subject to the condition F(S) ∼= ψ(S)[r(S)] for all S ∈SA . Here
SA and SB denote the sets of isomorphism classes of simple objects in A and B, respectively.
We show that a simple-shifted equivalence ensures that the underlying abelian categories are
equivalent (see Proposition 3.6), and moreover that a simple-shifted monoidal equivalence en-
sures that the underlying multiring categories are tensor equivalent (see Proposition 3.8). We
then investigate which tensor categories have the property that every monoidal derived equiv-
alence is necessarily simple-shifted. Along the way, we found that rigidity imposes additional
constraints on monoidal derived equivalences; see, for example, Lemma 4.1. Our main result
is Theorem 4.8, which states that:
Theorem 1.1. Let C and D be tensor categories, and let F : Db(C ) →Db(D) be a monoidal
derived equivalence. Write C ∼= L
γ∈Γ C γ, where each C γ is a block of C . Suppose that, for

(i) For every simple object S ∈C γ, there exists a tensor subcategory ES of C with finitely

derived equivalence between pointed tensor categories implies a tensor equivalence between
them (see Proposition 4.2).

3. Tensor equivalences from simple-shifted monoidal derived equivalences
In this section, we first introduce the definition of a simple-shifted equivalence between
bounded derived categories, and prove that every simple-shifted monoidal derived equivalence
between monoidal derived categories gives rise to a tensor equivalence in the sense of [12,
Definition 4.2.5].
Definition 3.1. Let A and B be abelian categories. A simple-shifted functor from Db(A )

all S ∈SA . We call (F, ψ, r) an equivalence if F is an equivalence of triangulated categories.
