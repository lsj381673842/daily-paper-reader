ADDITIVE INVARIANTS OF FINITE-DIMENSIONAL
DG-ALGEBRAS
ISAMBARD GOODBODY
Abstract. Orlov has shown that additive invariants such as K-theory and Hochschild
homology of smooth finite-dimensional DG-algebras behave similarly to those of finite-
dimensional algebras. The similarities only go so far: there are examples of finite-
dimensional DG-algebras with vanishing and uncountable Grothendieck groups. We
show that for DG-quivers with relations, the Grothendieck groups are free of the
expected rank and that, in the smooth case, all additive invariants are determined by
the base field. We use our results to rule out certain finite-dimensional DG-algebras
from being phantoms.

1. Introduction
In [Orl20], the Grothendieck group of a smooth finite-dimensional DG-algebra was
shown to be free and of finite but unknown rank. The goal of this note was to address
the following problem.
Problem 1.1 ([Orl20, Problem 2.22]). Find the rank of the Grothendieck group of
smooth finite-dimensional DG-algebras.
The Grothendieck group of a finite-dimensional algebra (smooth or not) is free of rank
given by the number of indecomposable projectives or equivalently the number of sim-
ples. The Grothendieck group of a finite-dimensional DG-algebra can behave very dif-
ferently: there are smooth finite-dimensional DG-algebras with vanishing Grothendieck
groups (Section 6) and there are finite-dimensional DG-algebras whose Grothendieck
groups are uncountable (Remark 3.1). To prove some positive results we focus on the
following class of finite-dimensional DG-algebras.

2. Preliminaries
Throughout k will denote a field. We let Ae := A ⊗k Aop denote the enveloping
algebra of A. The derived category of A will be denoted D(A), the perfect complexes
Dperf(A) and the modules with finite-dimensional cohomology Dfd(A). The following
notions are noncommutative analogues of smooth and proper schemes. See [Orl16] for
results making this precise.
Definition 2.1. Let A be a DG-algebra.
(1) A is smooth if A ∈Dperf(Ae).
(2) A is proper if H∗(A) is finite-dimensional.

Remark 2.2. Every finite-dimensional DG-algebra is clearly proper but the converse
is not true (e.g. [Goo24, Remark 2.4]). Recall that a DG-algebra A is connective if
Hi(A) = 0 for i > 0 and coconnective if Hi(A) = 0 for i < 0. By [Goo26, Appendix

a finite-dimensional DG-algebra. It follows from [KN13], that any regular proper co-
connective DG-algebra with semi-simple H0 is quasi-isomorphic to a finite-dimensional
DG-algebra.
Remark 2.3. By [Orl20, Corollary 2.20], finite-dimensional DG-algebras are exactly
those proper DG-algebras which admit categorical resolutions with full exceptional
collections.
In particular, a projective scheme X is derived equivalent to a finite-
dimensional DG-algebra if and only if it admits such a categorical resolution. This is
conjecturally related to rationality of X (see [Orl20, Conjecture 3.6], [LZ26], [Wei16]).
In particular, if X is a rational curve, it follows from [BDG17] or [KL15] (see also
[Wei16, Proposition 4.1]) that Dperf(C) is equivalent the perfect derived category of a
finite-dimensional DG-algebra.

Remark 3.3. Recall that a subcategory of a triangulated category is thick if it is
closed under extensions, shifts and direct summands. If A is any DG-algebra, then A
generates Dperf(A) as a thick subcategory. If A is a finite-dimensional algebra, then the
indecomposable summands of A generate Dperf(A) as a triangulated category i.e. it is
not necessary to take retracts. The following result generalises this fact.
Theorem 3.4. Let A be a finite-dimensional DG-algebra such that d(e) = 0 for every
idempotent e. The perfect derived category Dperf(A) is generated by the indecomposable
summands of A as a triangulated category and there is a surjection
ZNproj ↠K0(A).
Proof. The existence of the filtration in Lemma 3.2 implies every object of Dperf(A)
can be built from the indecomposable summands of A using extensions and shifts (and
without taking retracts). It follows that the class of any object in K0(A) is a linear
combination of the classes of the indecomposable summands of A.
□

of A/J+ generate Dperf(A) as a triangulated category.

6. Phantoms
A phantom is a smooth and proper DG-algebra such that all of its additive invariants
vanish. This is equivalent to the vanishing of its endomorphism ring in the category
of noncommutative motives in the sense of [Tab15]. Hence a smooth and proper DG-
algebra A is a phantom if and only if K0(Ae) = 0.
Remark 6.1. It follows from [Efi23, Theorem 6.2], that every phantom is quasi-
isomorphic to a finite-dimensional DG-algebra. This is a surprising result which dis-
proves [Orl20, Conjecture 3.7] in the strongest possible way. See [Kra24], [LS26] for the
simplest examples of phantoms.
Proposition 6.2. Let A be a finite-dimensional DG-algebra. Then A is a phantom if
and only if K0(A) = 0.
Proof. If A is a smooth finite-dimensional DG-algebra such that K0(A) = 0, then it
satisfies condition iii) of [Efi23, Theorem 6.2] by [Orl20, Theorem 3.3]. So condition ii)
holds and [A] ∈K0(Ae) is in the image of K0(A)⊗K0(Aop) = 0 and so [A] = 0. But [A]
corresponds to the identity morphism in the category motives as in [Tab15]. Therefore
Uadd(A) = 0 and A is a phantom.
□
Remark 6.3. Parts 1), and 2) of Theorem 1.4 follow immediately from Theorem 4.4
and Remark 4.3.
