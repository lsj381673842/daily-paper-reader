Locally Gorenstein algebras,
a generalization of Gorenstein algebras
Leonardo Alarcon, María Andrea Gatica, Marcelo Lanzilotta
Abstract
For an Artin algebra Λ, we define the class of locally Gorenstein modules, which is a generaliza-
tion of semi-Gorenstein projective modules and, therefore, a generalization of projective Gorenstein
modules. For a locally Gorenstein module X ∈mod Λ we construct χX a subcategory of mod Λ
and we prove that the syzygy functor is faithful and full on χX.
Using that, we prove that the
Igusa-Todorov functions ϕ and ψ vanish on the family of locally Gorenstein modules. Finally, using
locally Gorenstein modules, we define the locally Gorenstein algebras, a generalization of Gorenstein
algebras, and we prove that their finitistic, and the ϕ and ψ dimensions are finite.
Keywords: Semi-Gorenstein modules,Locally Gorenstein modules, Locally Gorenstein algebras, Finitistic
dimension, Igusa-Todorov functions, Morita context algebra.

Introduction
In this work Λ will denote an Artin algebra over a commutative artinian ring with unity R, where mod Λ
will denote the category of finitely generated left modules. In addition modΛ will denote the quotient of
mod Λ by the ideal of morphisms which factorize by projectives modules in mod Λ.
The concept of Gorenstein projective module (see 2.1) goes back to a work of Auslander and Bridger
in 1969 [2], where the G-dimension of a finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring has been
introduced. Enochs and Jenda in 1995 [7] defined the concept of Gorenstein projective module, and now
a day is clear that a module M is Gorenstein projective if and only if the G-dimension of M is zero.
Among the many properties that Gorenstein projective modules satisfy, we highlight the following
two: on the one hand, Gorenstein projective modules are semi-Gorenstein projective modules, that is,
they belong to ⊥Λ (see 2.2) and, on the other hand, they vanish for the Igusa-Todorov functions (see 2.2
and [14]).
In this work we introduce a new concept locally Gorenstein projective modules (see Definition 3.1)
that generalize semi-Gorenstein projective modules (and therefore also Gorenstein projective modules)
but, nevertheless, the Igusa - Todorov functions still have a value of zero on this family of modules (see
Theorem 2).
It is worth noting that to prove Theorem 2 we use a construction similar to that used by Auslander and
Bridge in [2], namely, starting from a locally Gorenstein projective module we construct a full subcategory
of modΛ, which we denote by χX, and we prove that the functor Ωrestricted to χX is faithful and full
(see Theorem 1).
From the locally Gorenstein projective modules, we define the locally Gorenstein algebras (see Def-
inition 5.1) by generalizing the concept of Gorenstein algebras defined by Auslander and Reiten (see
[3]). Gorenstein algebras have very nice homological behaviors, their homological dimensions: finitistic
dimension, ϕ-dimension and ψ-dimension are finite, and therefore satisfy the finitistic Conjecture. These
properties are still preserved in the generalization to locally Gorenstein algebras (see Proposition 5.4
and Theorem 3). It is worth mentioning at this point that there are at least, two families of algebras
which also generalize Gorenstein algebras. We are referring to virtually Gorenstein algebras, defined by
Beligiannis and Reiten in [5]; and weakly Gorenstein algebras, defined by Ringel and Zhang in [18]. In
a recent paper, Li is said to have shown that every virtually Gorenstein algebra is weakly Gorenstein
(see [16]). The new family of algebras that we define, locally Gorenstein algebras, does not coincide with

Igusa-Todorov functions
In this section we recall the definitions and some properties of the functions defined by K. Igusa and G.

Let K0 be the quotient of the free abelian group generated by the symbols of the form [M], where
M ∈mod Λ, by the subgroup generated by
1. [A] −[B] −[C] if A ∼= C ⊕B, with A, B, C in mod Λ.
2. [P] if P is a projective Λ-module.
Note that K0 is the free abelian group generated by the isomorphic classes of non-projective in-

Ω([M]) = [Ω(M)] for all [M] in K0.

Proposition 2.4. [13] Let M and N be modules in mod Λ, then the following statements hold:
1. If pd(M) < ∞then ϕ(M) = pd(M).
2. If M is indecomposable with pd(M) = ∞, then ϕ(M) = 0.
3. ϕ(M) ≤ϕ(M ⊕N).

The n-locally Gorenstein algebras
The homological dimensions defined with the Igusa-Todorov functions are related with the finitistic
dimension and global dimension. Indeed, the following inequalities are satisfied:
fin.dim(Λ) ≤ϕ-dim(Λ) ≤ψ-dim(Λ) ≤gl.dim(Λ).
5.1
Definition, examples and firsts results
In this section we define the n-locally Gorenstein algebras, and we calculate the finitistic dimension, the
ϕ-dimension and the ψ-dimension for this family of algebras. Finally, we exhibit examples of families of
n-locally Gorenstein algebras.
Definition 5.1. An Artin algebra Λ will be called n-locally Gorenstein if each module X in Ωn(mod Λ)
is locally Gorenstein, that is, for all X ∈Ωn(mod Λ) we have that Extm
