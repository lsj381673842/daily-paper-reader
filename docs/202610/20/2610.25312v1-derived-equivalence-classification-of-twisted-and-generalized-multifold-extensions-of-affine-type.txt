DERIVED EQUIVALENCE CLASSIFICATION OF TWISTED AND
GENERALIZED MULTIFOLD EXTENSIONS OF AFFINE TYPE
HIDETO ASASHIBA
Abstract. Let B be a piecewise hereditary algebra of affine type over an algebraically
closed field. In this paper we give a derived and stable equivalence classification of
twisted multifold extensions of B. The types eD and eE are already covered by the
classification for tree type. We therefore consider the remaining types eAp,q. We show
that the nth power of the Nakayama functor on the full subcategory of a transjective
component determines the twist, including its action on morphisms, up to conjugacy in
the derived Picard group of a hereditary algebra. Together with the hereditary derived
type and the folding number, this gives a complete invariant for derived equivalence,
and also for stable triangle equivalence. We then compute these conjugacy classes in
the unbalanced, balanced, bypass, and Kronecker cases, keeping the finite rotation data.
Finally, by the normalization theorem of Asashiba–Kimura–Nakashima–Yoshiwaki, we
obtain the same classification for generalized multifold extensions with nonzero integer
jump.
1. Introduction and the classification theorem
The derived and stable equivalence classification of twisted multifold extensions of
piecewise hereditary algebras of tree type was established in [1]. Its type consists of the
underlying tree, the folding number, and a conjugacy class of graph automorphisms.
The affine diagrams f

The remaining affine case is an acyclic orientation of a cycle. In this case the action on
the vertices of the Auslander–Reiten quiver does not, in general, determine the twist:
an additional parameter can occur in the action on morphisms.

The third component of our invariant is a conjugacy class, not merely the order of a
representative. Even for graph automorphisms, two elements of the same order need
not be conjugate. For example, the automorphism group of the graph f
D4 is S4, acting
on its four outer vertices. The permutations (12) and (12)(34) both have order two,
but they are not conjugate. Thus replacing the conjugacy class by the order would lose
information needed for the classification.
A further point occurs in the balanced case p = q = m > 1. Consider the twists whose
graph part is a rotation. Write their parameters as (λ, a), where λ ∈k× is the scalar
parameter and a ∈Z/mZ specifies the power ra of a rotation r of order m. As shown
in Theorem 6.1, two such pairs represent the same conjugacy class precisely when they
are equal or are related by
(λ, a) 7−→(λ−1, −a).
The scalar and the rotation are therefore inverted simultaneously, not independently.
For example, over C with m = 3, the pair (2, 1) is identified with (1/2, 2), but with
neither (2, 2) nor (1/2, 1). Consequently, recording the scalar up to inversion and the
rotation up to inversion separately would identify distinct conjugacy classes.

polynomial growth, or a specified representation type. The classification concerns this
family of extensions.
