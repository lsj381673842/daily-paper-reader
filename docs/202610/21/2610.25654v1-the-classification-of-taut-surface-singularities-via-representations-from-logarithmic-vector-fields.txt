THE CLASSIFICATION OF TAUT SURFACE SINGULARITIES VIA REPRESENTATIONS FROM LOGARITHMIC VECTOR FIELDS 

HEESANG PARK AND DONGSOO SHIN Abstract. We study logarithmic vector fields on exceptional cycles through finite-dimensional representations and recover Laufer’s classification of taut normal complex surface singularities. For graphs with two nodes, restriction maps define representations of a fixed quiver, with dimensions bounded independently of the number of components and their self-intersections. We identify restriction cohomology with an extension group and use interval decomposition to reduce its vanishing to interpolation on a rational chain. These calculations determine the classification conditions, and vanishing on every analytic realization proves sufficiency, including the nonrational cases. The obstruction cycles also give a uniform criterion: a graph is taut if and only if 𝐻 1 (5𝐸, 𝑆 𝑋 |5𝐸 ) = 0 for every analytic realization, where 𝑆 𝑋 is the ambient logarithmic tangent bundle. We prove that five is the smallest positive integer for which this criterion holds for all graphs. 

1. Introduction We study the cohomology of logarithmic vector fields on exceptional cycles by means of finite-dimensional representations. Let (𝑋, 𝐸 ) → ( 𝑉, 0) be the minimal good resolution of a normal complex surface singularity, with 𝐸 reduced, and put 𝑆 𝑋 = Θ 𝑋 (− log 𝐸 ).For resolution graphs with two nodes, we construct a positive exceptional cycle 𝑍 and decompose it into pieces on which the first cohomology of the restricted logarithmic tangent bundle vanishes. The restriction maps on their intersections define a representation of a fixed quiver, whose dimension is bounded independently of the number of exceptional components and their self-intersections. We identify 𝐻 1 (𝑍, 𝑆 𝑋 |𝑍 ) with an extension group over the path algebra of this quiver. Interval decomposition expresses its dimension as a sum of three multiplicities. When a nonempty connecting chain remains, only one multiplicity contributes, and the calculation of the restriction images reduces its vanishing to interpolation on a chain of rational curves. These calculations give another proof of the classification of taut normal complex surface singularities due to Laufer [7]. Restriction to the reduced exceptional divisor first bounds the topology of a taut graph. The restrictions along the arms determine the one-node cases, and the representations and interpolation calculations determine the remaining conditions for graphs with two nodes. We prove sufficiency by extending cohomology vanishing from the chosen cycles to every analytic realization, including the nonrational cases. The necessity arguments also produce, for every excluded graph, an analytic realization with a cycle 𝑌 ≤ 5𝐸 such that 𝐻 1 (𝑌 , 𝑆 𝑋 |𝑌 ) ≠ 0. Consequently, a graph is taut if and only if 

𝐻 1 (5𝐸, 𝑆 𝑋 |5𝐸 ) = 0 for every analytic realization. An explicit example shows that five is the smallest positive integer for which this criterion holds for all graphs. 1.1. Main results. Let (𝑉, 0) be a normal complex surface singularity, and let 𝜋 : (𝑋, 𝐸 ) → (𝑉, 0) be its minimal good resolution, with 𝐸 reduced. A dual graph is taut if any two singularities with that minimal good resolution graph are analytically isomorphic. We work over C throughout the paper.

Section 2 reduces the possible graphs to chains, cycles, and trees with at most two trivalent nodes, and proves tautness for chains and cycles. Section 3 classifies the one-node graphs by restriction to the central curve and the arms, together with the weighted homogeneous calculation and Wahl’s vanishing theorem. Sections 4 and 5 construct the two-node cycles, compute their restriction images, and derive the remaining conditions by interval decomposition and interpolation. Section 6 extends vanishing from these cycles to every analytic realization. When 𝐻 1 (𝑍, O𝑍 ) = 0, nef twists and formal functions extend the vanishing to 𝑋 . The exceptional nonrational two-node family is treated by exact sequences on two reduced rational trees. Finally, the nonvanishing cycles used in the necessity proofs can be chosen inside 5 𝐸 , giving Theorem II; Example 6.8 proves optimality.
