> Abstract. We study the 2-representation theory of the Taft category, the tensor category of finite-dimensional modules over the Taft algebra. This category has two left cells. We first classify suitable transitive modules over its based representation ring. For the upper cell, we recall a construction by Etingof and Ostrik giving an infinite family of pairwise nonequivalent simple 2-representations. For the bottom cell, we pass to the semisimplification of the Taft category and identify a distinguished fusion subcategory with an adjoint sl 2
> Verlinde category. Using algebra objects associated with ADET -diagrams and categorical Clifford theory, we classify the regular exact indecomposable module categories of this semisimplification. For these regular module categories, the split Grothendieck group is a complete invariant. Finally, we identify the two cell 2-representations of the Taft category.

1. Introduction 

This article is a first step towards a complete description of the categorical representation theory of the Taft algebra based on the cell theory of 2-representations, developed in [24, 25]. The Taft algebra, originally defined in [36], is a finite-dimensional non-semisimple algebra, which has the advantage of having an explicit but non-trivial representation theory. At the same time, it exhibits many features characteristic of nonsemisimple algebras. The Taft algebra is a Hopf algebra that can be identified with the Borel part of the small quantum group of sl 2 at roots of unity [18, Lemma 8.4]. Moreover, this algebra is not unimodular. The tensor category of finite-dimensional Taft modules, which we call the Taft category, has a unique nonzero proper thick tensor ideal generated by all projective modules [32, Theorem 5.5]. Another special feature of this algebra is that it has only one-dimensional simple modules. From the perspective of cell 2-representation theory, the Taft category has only two cells, which we refer to as the bottom and the upper cell. The upper cell is mainly controlled by projective modules, whereas the bottom cell is associated with the stable category and its semisimplification. Thus, the Taft category provides a good example of a tensor category that is not a fusion category and whose categorical representation theory can be described concretely. Take n ∈ Z≥2, and K an algebraically closed field whose characteristic does not divide n.Fix a primitive nth root of unity ζ ∈ K. By default, all categories and functors are assumed to be additive, K-linear and all monoidal categories will be strict (thanks to Mac Lane’s coherence theorem). The main results of this article are the following. First, we classify certain transitive modules over the representation ring of the Taft algebra associated with the upper and bottom cells. These modules arise as decategorifications of the module categories considered later in the article.

Proof. By Lemma 5.10, we have U( An,λ ) ∼= P1. Since Sr ⊗ P1 ∼= P1+ r, for any r = 0 , . . . , n 9 2, we can use Theorem 5.12 to see that the indecomposable objects in ℳ𝒯 (An,λ ) are precisely the indecomposable projective T-modules. However, it is not equivalent to 𝒫 r( 𝒯 ), because 

ℳ𝒯 (An,λ ) is semisimple by Corollary 5.13. □

Let us conclude this section by identifying the cell 2-representation of the bottom cell Cb

with the semisimplification of the Taft category. 

Proposition 8.6. The cell 2-representation LCb is equivalent to 𝒯 , as 2-representation of 

𝒯 .

Proof. Thanks to Lemma 3.17, it is enough to prove that 𝒵 (≥) = 𝒩 , the tensor ideal of negligible morphisms of 𝒯 . Indeed, 𝒯 being a fusion category, it is a simple 2-representation of 𝒯 , hence a simple 2-representation of 𝒯 . Moreover 𝒩 does not contain the identify morphism of any object of Cb. We thus get the desired equality. □
